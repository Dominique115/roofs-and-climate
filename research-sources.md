## Photo collection

22 Commons photographs now show roof details, village context, and additional viewpoints. Every image has its own photographer, original source page and licence in `galleries.json`, and visible credits in the explorer. The gallery may show different buildings in the same settlement rather than the same building from multiple angles. Eight new photographs were inspected for relevance before inclusion. Photographs are embedded in the HTML for offline viewing.

## Evidence still missing

No specific seasonal explanation was established for Holasovice. Glaumbaer remains without a seasonal narrative: the existence of turf construction alone is not enough to assign a cooling or insulation benefit to a particular month. Bermuda's reflective-roof explanation remains labelled as a general mechanism rather than a local performance measurement.


## Collection expansion and seasonal photographs

Three locations added with the same NASA POWER 1991–2020 monthly climate baseline:

- Seongeup, Jeju: [Jeju Tourism Organization](https://ijto.or.kr/english/?cid=22) and [Korea Tourism Organization travel guide](https://english1.visitkorea.or.kr/e_book/access/ecatalogt.jsp?Dir=913&callmode=normal&catimage=&eclang=ko&start=114&um=s) explain rope-secured roofs in response to wind and rain. No gust resistance is estimated.
- Apia area, Samoa: [UNESCO educational resource, printed p. 248](https://whc.unesco.org/document/168061#page=249) describes open-sided ventilation and weather blinds. The photographed building has a metal roof, labelled as a contemporary variation; no material cooling claim is made.
- Hollywood Reservation, Florida: [Florida Seminole Tourism](https://www.floridaseminoletourism.com/seminole-spaces-chickees/) describes palm-thatch rainfall protection and summer airflow. The selected historical photograph dates to 1960, whereas the climate baseline is 1991–2020.

Shirakawa-go now includes an authenticated winter image photographed on February 17, 2006 by tsuda, alongside two October 24, 2016 views by Gilles Desjardins. Seasonal labels describe the photographs, not the long-term monthly averages. The images show local examples, not a repeated survey of the same building. The winter image visibly shows snow remaining on the roofs, consistent with the existing explanation that steep roofs do not eliminate all snow accumulation. A button in the winter narrative selects the winter photograph.
